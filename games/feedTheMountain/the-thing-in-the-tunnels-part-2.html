Reconciliation

Field Report 02 — Chalet Ridgemont, Swiss Alps

Head office sent an auditor.

This was, on paper, the single most predictable thing that could have happened, given that Julian had spent the better part of a week feeding actual livestock into a load-bearing crevasse and calling it "asset reallocation." The auditor's name was Griggs, he arrived by helicopter looking extremely unprepared for altitude, and he stepped off the landing pad holding a briefcase like it contained the last known copy of anything.

"Mr. Voss," Griggs said, extending a hand. "I understand there's been some inventory drift."

"Drift," Julian repeated warmly, shaking it, "is such a harsh word for seasonal recalibration."

Method growled, low and long.

"He says," Dez translated, not looking up from her chocolate, "that you should probably not let go of the briefcase."

"Why on earth not?" Griggs said, already setting it down on the crate to fish out a tablet.

The mountain answered for him. Somewhere below, a sound like a held breath finally letting go, and the briefcase — full of Julian's very carefully assembled numbers, the actual proof — simply wasn't there anymore. Griggs blinked at the empty patch of crate like it had personally betrayed him.

"That's—" he said. "That's impossible, I set it down two seconds ago—"

"Two seconds is a long time to not be paying attention to something," Dez said, unwrapping another truffle. "Welcome to the mountain. It reads intent."

Here was the part nobody had prepared for: the thing in the tunnels had gotten smarter. Not smarter like it was learning calculus. Smarter like it had started paying attention to what got attention — and had begun making decoys.

The first one showed up disguised as chocolate. Brynlee, delighted at finally having a safe thing to interact with, picked it up, and it dissolved into something that was absolutely not chocolate, was in fact extremely cold and extremely wrong, and she made a noise previously undocumented in either the human or canine vocal range.

"Okay, NEW RULE," Brynlee announced, vibrating on top of her crate. "We check the chocolate now. We authenticate the chocolate."

"With what," Dez said. "A notary?"

"With CONFIDENCE, Dez, we authenticate it with confidence—"

Method barked once, sharp.

"He says it's mimicking the safe stuff on purpose now," Dez said. "Because we've been rewarding chocolate. It noticed. It's optimizing."

Julian, to his credit, recovered admirably fast for a man who had just lost his entire alibi to a geological event. He turned back to Griggs with the same smile, unbothered, unbent. "As you can see," he said, gesturing at the empty crate like a magician mid-trick, "the numbers audit themselves out here. Very efficient system."

"You're standing in front of a monster," Griggs said.

"I'm standing in front of an opportunity," Julian said. "There's a meaningful difference, and it's mostly branding."

Method growled again — long, unimpressed, almost tired.

"He says," Dez said, "that you are, and I'm quoting the growl directly here, going to be next."

Griggs did not take this well. Griggs, in fact, immediately began doing the one thing everyone in the bunker had unanimously agreed not to do: paying enormous, undivided, terrified attention to absolutely everything at once — the walls, the floor, the ceiling, his own hands — in the specific way people do right before they start making decisions based on adrenaline instead of information.

"Don't do that," Brynlee said, surprising everyone including herself. "The staring thing. It's worse. You gotta pick one thing to actually watch and let the rest go, or you're not paying attention to anything, you're just panicking with your eyes open." She said this while holding a fresh chocolate at arm's length and inspecting it with genuine, hard-won suspicion. "Trust me. I've been doing this since Tuesday. I'm basically tenured."

It was, Dez noted silently, the first useful sentence Brynlee had produced all week, and possibly ever.

Griggs picked the door. He watched the door. His breathing slowed by exactly one degree.

Below them, the mountain — having just learned that fear tasted different from neglect, richer somehow, more filling — settled in to wait and see which one the bunker would run out of first.

Julian's briefcase, notably, has still not resurfaced. Neither has Griggs' original tablet. Both, according to Method, are "compromised." Nobody has asked him to elaborate.
